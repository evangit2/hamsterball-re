Chapter 1: Rescue Force Academy 
________________ 
 
 
‘It’s today!!! It's finally today!!!’ 
The thought raced through Grace’s mind like a lightning bolt, electrifying her out of bed. By the time her watch alarm went off not even five seconds later, she had already made it over to her dresser and was partway through combing the traces of bedhead out of her fur. Reflexively, the border collie switched off the piercing beeping. It wouldn't help her get ready any faster. 
Today was one of the biggest days of her life. 
It was the first day of college, and not just any college: her dream college. Rescue Force Academy. 
Rescue Force Academy was - paws down - the best school she could possibly get into for her degree, and it was the most prestigious too. It was one of the best in the entire nation, and everyone who graduated from it became major pillars of society. In fact, the school was entirely run by donations from alumni, which meant admission was free. 
But that was where the catch came in. Because of the unique way the academy financially operated, it only accepted fifteen new students every year. Each year, the board of directors would sit down and plow through lists of graduating seniors from across the nation. They would handpick students who all had a specific area of expertise and who were the best of their class. Then, they would sift out all but fifteen of those students: the students who they thought would have the most positive impact on the country. These fifteen students would make up the incoming class at RFA. 
Grace was not one of those fifteen students. 
She was the sixteenth. 
For the first time in the school’s fifty years of existence, the school had bent the rules explicitly for her. After already solidifying their fifteen picks, they had decided to enroll her as well. 
That was why today was such an important day. 
As she left her room for the last time, she swiped her backpack off the hook by the door. After closing the door behind her, she crept down the stairwell, avoiding every creaky step as best she could. It was four in the morning and everyone else in the building was still asleep - especially after the goodbye party last night. At the front door, Grace reached into her backpack and grabbed an envelope stuffed to bursting with farewell notes. Slowly, deliberately, she rose on her hind paws and adhered it to the inside of the front door. Then she dropped back down on all fours. 
It was time. 
Taking one last glance behind her, she soaked in everything she could lay eyes on, letting her earliest childhood memories play on repeat. Then she stopped, focusing on forming a new memory. This could be the last time she ever saw this orphanage again. She should make the most of it. 
Finally, the border collie turned back towards the door, opening it into the dark, lamplit street beyond. 
“Miss Grace?” a sleepy voice behind her said. 
Grace closed her eyes and sighed before turning back around with a small smile. As she expected, a little English bulldog puppy sat on the bottom step, his head tilted to one side. 
“Well, hello there. What are you doing awake? Don't tell me you set an alarm just to see me again,” Grace whispered, moving towards him. 
“No! No! I didn't!” the pup denied. A lie. 
Grace could tell he was lying, but decided not to call him out. She always had the uncanny ability to tell whether someone was telling the truth or not. Instead, she simply walked over and gave him a hug. “Do you want a glass of water?” 
He hesitated, but he nodded, so the border collie led him to the kitchen. She took a glass and filled it at the sink. When she gave it to her small friend, he drank the glass in one go. 
“Woah, slow down,” Grace cautioned, giving a small laugh. She took the glass back and set it in the sink. 
“Now you need to go back to bed. I’m going to go now, okay?” 
Suddenly, the bulldog clung to her leg. “I don’t want you to go, Miss Grace! Can’t you stay here?” 
Grace looked down at him. Both of their eyes were brimming with tears. This was one of the hardest goodbyes. 
“I can’t stay, but I’ll be back before you know it. I left you something by the door, and you can have somebody help you read it in the morning.” 
She pulled away from the hug and gave a wink. “If you read really closely, it might tell you where to look for Mr. Carrot.” 
The pup’s brown eyes widened. “Really?” 
Grace nodded. “But I need you to do me a favor. With everybody asleep, I have nobody to wave to. Can you watch me walk away at the window in the boys’ bedroom so I can wave at you?” 
His tail wagged a little. “Yeah. I’ll wave to you too.” 
Grace tried to hold back her tears again. “Good. But then you need to go to bed so that you’ll get big, strong, and well-rested to be the best builder ever.” 
He hugged Grace again, saying, “And then I’ll go to school with you!” 
She kissed him on his little, furry forehead and ushered him up the stairs, whispering, “You’re a very tough pup. I love you, Rubble.” 
Once his tail was out of sight, she opened the door once more and stepped outside. Resolutely, the canine set off for the train station. She had a long journey ahead of her, but for now, she turned around and looked at the little figure in the upstairs window and walked and waved until she could see him no longer. 
________________ 
 
 
Two and a half hours later, the world was finally starting to wake up. The sun had just barely peeked its face over the horizon, and other dogs and cats were finally milling about. The train brakes screeched to a halt and Grace bounded out of the car. 
To a normal passerby, she didn’t appear out of the ordinary. Though she was abnormally small for a border collie, nobody would have been able to notice. She had a very normal fur pattern for her breed, though it was clear she took good care of her coat. It was shining and neatly brushed. Even her black fur glistened in the early morning sunlight. A white stripe traveled from the bridge of her nose and down her chin. The stripe spread into a circle that encompassed her neck before covering her stomach. Additionally, her paws, the tips of her ears, and the end of her tail were also white. Over her shoulders she wore her backpack; a dark, leather bag which held all her meager worldly possessions. 
As Grace headed for the nearest bus stop, she glanced over at one of the newspaper dispensers nearby. “WAR STILL LOOMS ON HORIZON” the headline blasted in bold. Grace sighed. She had a long journey ahead of her. 
Soon, the bus Grace had been waiting on arrived. She hopped on, paid her fare, and took a seat in a lonely aisle, sliding her backpack onto the seat next to her. Normally she might try to start a conversation with the people around her, but not today. She had a long day ahead of her, and she was so… so… tired… 
________________ 
 
 
Grace jerked awake. 
She had fallen asleep. 
“No no no…” she muttered, trying to wipe the tiredness out of her eyes. She could have sworn she had only nodded for a moment - a moment… And yet, she found herself in a completely unfamiliar bedroom. So much for a good first impression… 
‘Wait… bedroom?’ 
Despite the growing desire to roll over and go back to sleep, Grace slowly pushed off the covers and dropped onto her four paws. It was a nicer bedroom than any she had ever slept in before. It had a dresser with a mirror opposite the bed, and a small coffee table rested in the middle of the room. A small lightstand with a lamp on it was next to the head of the bed, and Grace noticed with relief that her backpack was leaning against it. An empty coatrack was hung on the wall near the entrance door. The door itself was a metallic white, like the walls. Everything was clean, spotless, and pristine. But none of these attributed to Grace’s awe more than the full, private bathroom that was attached to the bedroom. 
She shook her head to clear it, sending her ears flapping wildly. Now wasn't the time to gawk. She still had no idea where she was. 
The border collie walked towards the front door. She paused in mild surprise when it automatically opened, sliding into the wall with a hiss. Wherever she was, it was very luxurious. Logging this in her thoughts, the canine exited the room and started exploring. She appeared to be at the start of a long, sleek, carpeted hallway full of similar rooms. Each of them had a placard placed on the door at eye-level: Skye, Shade, Rex… names. 
Did her door have one too? 
She ran back to her door and glanced at the sign. 
Grace. 
And in the background behind it, a shield with a paw print on it: the crest for Rescue Force Academy. 
Her heart leapt in her stomach and a grin broke out on her muzzle. She was really actually here!!! But she had no clue what time it was. She had budgeted time to arrive an hour early so she would definitely be on time, but she had no clue how much of that time had passed. She had to find everyone. 
Faster than a cheetah, Grace sped out of the hallway, following the twists and turns of the corridors until the hallway opened into the largest room she had ever seen. 
The great hall’s ceiling was about twice the height the hallways permitted, giving the room a welcoming, open feeling. The floor seemed to be made of white marble, though upon closer inspection proved to be a special alloy of metal Grace wasn't familiar with. The walls and ceiling were a similar white and were almost completely bare. You could just barely make out outlines where pictures had been hung in years past. The only thing remaining was a large silver RFA emblem hung above a hallway on the right wall. On Grace’s left, the wall was almost completely taken up by a giant door. It was designed similarly to the rest of the doors in the building, and was clearly made for function rather than fashion. It looked more like an airlock or a vault door, even though it was clearly the front entrance. Hallways branched off directly ahead and to Grace’s right as well. But all of these observations were second-hand to the canine. The majority of her attention was fixated on the group of people in front of her. 
There seemed to be approximately ten people in the room, though more were filing in. A quick glance showed a Great Dane excitedly talking to a pair of golden retrievers. A Siberian husky was chatting nonchalantly with a Portuguese water dog. Nearby, a blue mackerel tabby cat was chatting with a Bernese mountain dog in a wheelchair. Off on his own, a German shepherd stood alert in expectant silence, watching as every new person entered. 
Grace strode confidently forward and joined the nearest group: the one with the golden retrievers and the Great Dane. They smiled at her as she approached, setting their conversation aside to let her join. 
“Hi! How are you? What's your name? Are you excited?” the Great Dane asked in a single breath. His honey-gold form positively towered over Grace, as his breed normally did, but even then, his paws and muzzle looked too large for him, indicating that he would grow much bigger than even now. Grace was silently thankful that he was friendly, or else he would have been a truly formidable classmate to become acquainted with. 
“Absolutely I'm excited!” she cheered, matching his energy. “My name is Grace. What are your names?” 
“My name’s Dwayne!” the Great Dane barked. 
“And I'm Ella, and this is my brother, Tuck,” the female golden retriever softly answered with a smile. She didn't have nearly the same energy that Dwayne did, but didn't seem overwhelmed by it. 
“Nice to meetchya!” Tuck cordially replied, shaking Grace’s paw enthusiastically. “Always great to meet another classmate!” Looking between the two siblings, it was obvious they were twins. Other than a small white bandaid on Ella’s left cheek and the backpack that she wore, the only thing that differentiated them was a single strand of fur they each had dyed on the tops of their heads. Ella’s was red, whereas Tuck’s was a light blue. 
“Say, do any of you know who found me and carried me into my room? I fell asleep on the bus and found myself here.” Grace claimed. 
“Nope, sorry,” Tuck shook his head. “Ella and I actually had a similar thing happen to us. We live pretty far away, and today was a pretty early morning, so we stayed the night at a hotel not far from here. We both woke up in our rooms here about half an hour ago. All our stuff was here too.” 
“Huh. Same thing happened to me!” Dwayne exclaimed. 
“We figured it was probably part of initiation,” Ella reasoned. 
“Ah, yes! Kidnapping your students the night before they're supposed to arrive is such a great way to start your first day at school!” Grace joked, earning some laughter from the others. Then she switched topics. “By the way, none of you would happen to have a watch, would you?” 
“It’s one minute ‘til eight,” a new, deep voice cut in from behind. Turning around, Grace watched as a light grey dog walked towards them from the bedroom hallway. He was the antithesis of “picturesque.” His left ear was pointed and had a nick in it while his right one hung limply. He wore a leather toolbelt around his waist laden with a variety of tools. His fur was unkempt and large dark splotches of fur were splattered haphazardly all over his body. One was directly centered over his left eye, giving the distinct impression that he had been socked in the face. Or maybe he had been…? To top it all off, he had one of the worst cases of bedhead that Grace had ever seen. 
“Almost eight…” Grace mused, looking down to mask her surprise slightly. She had slept almost a full hour. Then she looked back at the newcomer. “So orientation should be starting any moment, right?” 
“Sounds like it,” Tuck nodded. “I'm hoping they'll do introductions before we really get into anything. I haven't had nearly enough time to meet anyone.” 
As the golden retriever finished his sentence, the loudspeaker system crackled to life. “Attention all new students: Please meet in the Main Hall for introductions. Orientation is starting.” 
“Well, whaddaya know? Today's your lucky day,” the grey dog stated. Ella and Tuck shared a knowing grin. 
As the last of the students started filing in, a robotic dog seemed to suddenly appear in the room. It was built almost entirely out of metal and polished to a silvery finish. The only part of it that wasn't metal were its eyes. They were displayed on a screen of blue LED lights, which was protected behind a rounded plate of glass that took up the majority of the top half of the machine’s head. But the most striking thing was how fluid and lifelike it was. In fact, if it wasn't for the partially transparent head, Grace might have thought it was a dog wearing a highly advanced metal suit. The robot walked slowly, confidently towards the center of the room. As it did, everyone shifted their formations to create one big circle with the robot in the center. Then it spoke. 
“Helllllllloooo new recruits! Welcome to boot camp!” Its voice was cheery and pleasant, but commanding. The sudden voice was offsettingly full of life and emotion, given how it was coming from a robot. “My name is Axel, and I'll be the face of Rescue Force Academy as you settle in!” 
“Woah… are you a robot?” a cockapoo gasped. The strands of fur on her ears were dyed pink and blue, and around her neck she wore a necklace with a large, perfectly intact seashell. Her eyes sparked with excitement and anticipation. Next to her, another cockapoo - this one with normal cream-colored fur - rolled her eyes. 
If Axel was annoyed at the interruption, he didn't show it. “Good question!” he affirmed. “I am an android, which is a robot designed to be as lifelike as possible! Everything about me from my body to the AI core running me was designed and built by students just like you all!” 
“Cool!” a husky from across the room awed. 
Axel continued with his spiel. “Now, I'm sure you're all very excited and ready to begin the day, but first I want everyone to get to know each other on a first name basis, y’know? So I want everyone to go around and introduce themselves. Say your name, where you're from, what your ultimate talent is, and why you're here. Let's seeeee… who wants to go first?” 
As the robot looked around, deciding who would start, Grace scanned the room. Counting herself, there were only fifteen students here. Someone was absent. Thankfully, it seemed she wasn't the only one who realized this. 
“WAIT!!!” a voice shouted from across the room. “We're still missing someone!!!” Grace glanced over and noted that it was the German shepherd who had shouted out. 
“I don't know what you're whining about. There's fifteen of us here,” a ginger tabby to Grace’s left remarked brusquely. “We’ll figure it out later.” The German shepherd lowered his ears and glared, but maintained his silence. Grace tried to speak out, but found the words catching in her throat. How awkward would it be to have to admit that she was the exception? That, under normal circumstances, she shouldn’t be here? Best to let the robot explain that. 
Axel glanced around the room, processing the number of attendees. “Hmm… we do have fifteen students here. I don't know-” Then his eyes landed on Grace. He froze, as if processing something. His lifeless stare bored into her soul, and everyone followed his gaze. They could all tell who he was looking at. 
Grace was petrified. Why did it think there were only supposed to be fifteen? Had something gone wrong? Was she, in fact, not supposed to be here? Was it just a foolish pipe dream of hers to ever want to go to such an incredible school in the first place? 
Then Axel whirred back to life, blinking away his computational trance. Almost instantly he was back to his normal self. “Oh, I'm sorry, I have just received additional instructions. Everyone, this year, for the first time in history, we are having not fifteen, but sixteen students!” 
A number of gasps and murmurs went around the room. They knew how big of a deal this was. 
“What’s so special about her?” the cream colored cockapoo asked, narrowing her eyes. 
“If there's supposed to be sixteen of us, why are there only fifteen here?” the grey dog next to Grace asked. 
“Excellent question!” Axel exclaimed. “Let's see where our absent student in question is!” 
Faster than anyone could blink, a pair of wheels extended from the android’s paws, and he blasted into the hallway towards the dorms. Immediately, there was a shout of surprised protest. 
“AHH! HEY!” Apparently the student had been attempting to watch the proceedings from afar. 
Then Axel returned, shoving one more canine into the circle right between Grace and the ginger tabby on her left. He appeared to be a Labrador retriever with fur as pitch black as the dead of night, and he looked virtually petrified and extremely embarrassed. His ears were flat against the sides of his head and it looked like he was attempting to make himself as small as possible - a difficult task, considering how he was already considerably small for his breed. 
“Wait… but…” the German shepherd muttered. 
“There we go! We're all accounted for!” Axel proclaimed, dusting off the wheels on his paws before retracting them away. “Now, would you like to begin us with introductions?” He stared pointedly at the newcomer. 
“Uh… I… um…” the Lab looked decidedly sick. “I don't know what's going on,” he finally admitted. 
“It's okay. I can start,” the grey dog on Grace’s right offered. Axel nodded in silent approval. The grey dog cleared his throat before continuing. “My name is Rocky and I'm from Massachusetts. I was invited here as the Ultimate Mechanic after building a method for safe and renewable energy extraction from nuclear fusion reactors. It wasn't anything big, really. All I did was figure out a way to siphon out tritium from sulphur hexafluoride while also maintaining the standard… Oh, sorry,” he rubbed his head. Now his bedhead was worse than before. “I'm overexplaining.” 
Grace gulped. She was surprised he had only gotten the title of Ultimate Mechanic and not Ultimate Nuclear Engineer with that feat. It made her start to wonder if she had made a mistake in coming. If that was the type of thing that most of these people had done to get in, then she was way out of her league. 
Rocky glanced to his right, indicating the direction they would go around the circle. 
“Well, guess I'm next,” Tuck grinned. “Hi everyone! My name is Tuck. I'm from Ohio, and I'm here because I won a raffle.” 
A ripple of disbelieving laughter went around the circle, but Grace paused. There wasn't any insincerity to what he said or how he said it. But she decided to stay silent. 
“I'm serious,” Tuck pressed. “Apparently RFA actually does research on luck. Every year, the board members take all the names of the graduates they don't select and randomly choose one of them as the Ultimate Lucky Student. I just so happened to be that lucky guy.” He gave a winning grin and nodded to his sister. 
Grace breathed a sigh of relief. Maybe she was fine here after all. 
“Hi everyone, I'm Ella. If you can't tell, this dunderhead is my twin brother. And while he got all the good luck of the family, I got all the bad luck.” Ella gave her brother a playful nudge as she said it. “Don't ask me how they found out, but apparently they did, so now I'm here so they can study me as the ‘Ultimate Misfortunate Student.’ Yay.” 
Next, Dwayne took a step forward. “Hi fellas! I'm Dwayne! I'm from Louisiana and I'm here as… uh…” his face fell as he paused in deep thought. “Uhhhhh… I can't seem to remember… Come back to me later.” Grace narrowed her eyes. How on earth could anyone here forget the entire reason they were invited here in the first place?!? Unless of course they were the “Ultimate Airhead” or something like that. But it was obvious he wasn’t quite telling the truth. 
With a shrug, the next person began. This was a small tuxedo cat wearing a purple neckerchief around her neck. She was all black except for white patches on her paws, muzzle, and the end of her tail. She seemed cool and collected, though strategic when she spoke: almost like playing a game of chess, where her words were pieces. “Hi everyone, I'm Shade. I'm from Oak Ridge, Tennessee, and I'm here as the Ultimate Physicist. I was invited for helping calculate the physics that was needed to discover element 117.” 
Next, the cockapoo with dyed fur and the seashell necklace stepped forward. Her normal fur was a very light tan, though she had slightly darker patches around her eyes. “Hey everyone! I'm Coral! I live about twenty minutes north of Los Angeles, and I'm here as a marine biologist! I was invited for discovering and documenting four new species of marine life!” Grace nodded affirmatively, noting that her first impression seemed to be accurate: Coral had a very bubbly personality. 
After her, the other cockapoo with darker cream-colored fur stepped forward. On her left side she wore a small pink satchel, belted around her waist, and she held herself with a stiff posture. She seemed to be much less enamored about being in the spotlight, and as a result, her words were short and precise, though not entirely in an unlikable manner. “My name is Skye. I'm from Los Angeles, and I'm here as the Ultimate Aviator. Got my invitation for being the youngest stunt pilot ever.” 
After her, it was the German shepherd’s turn. He was mostly a deep, serious brown, but there were some parts that showed a softer tan. From the tip of his nose, arching over his eyes, and from his chest to the under tip of his tail were all tan along with his paws. The markings were incredibly symmetrical, giving him a sharp, serious air. His posture was tense and cold, which wasn't much of a surprise given how he had recently been shut down about there being a missing student. “My name is Chase. I'm from Kenosha, Wisconsin, and I'm training as the Ultimate SWAT Officer. I’m here because I stopped a riot single-pawed during the pandemic.” 
“Wait! That's right! I remember hearing about you on the news,” Ella awed. “I didn't realize you were our age though.” 
“How old did you think I was? Thirty-eight?” Chase snipped tersely. Apparently it was a sensitive topic. 
“Sorry… I just thought it was neat,” the golden retriever sighed. 
Next was the Portuguese water dog. He was a rich hickory color, but white was splotched messily on his underside, paws, and muzzle. A pair of black headphones hung around his neck, and he wore a pouch on his side strapped around his waist. The headphones and pouch shared a bright blue symbol on it that reminded Grace of an infinity symbol. He spoke very bluntly, as though each word was a chore to get over with. “I'm Moby from San Francisco and I’m the Ultimate Hacker.” That was all he said before looking at the husky on his right. 
The husky in question was quite a beautiful sight. The main color of her fur was a stunning indigo lavender, brought out by the white of her face and paws. She wore two turquoise satchels in a similar style to Moby’s, one on either side. Her tail was going a mile a minute as if she had been waiting to introduce herself her whole life. She started without need of any further prompting. “Hi! I'm Everest! I'm from Silicon Valley, and I'm the Ultimate Astronaut! Before you ask, I've never been to space yet, but I've done a ton of training and prep with the Ames Research Center. I've been working really closely with their astrobiology department over the past few years.” 
After the bubbly husky came a Labrador retriever. His fur color was a dark chocolate brown, and he had a pair of aviator sunglasses pulled up on his forehead. His demeanor was very chill, and it was hard for Grace to get a good read on him. “Sup, y’all! I'm Zuma, and I'm from Florida. I’m here as the Ultimate Diver. Got the title because I found the wreckage of Amelia Earhart's plane.” 
“That was you?” Skye’s jaw dropped almost all the way to the floor, and her demeanor completely changed. Zuma simply smirked in response. He probably got it a lot. 
Next came the blue mackerel tabby cat. He wore a pair of perfectly round glasses, perched low on his nose and a black business tie around his fluffy neck. He had a small pouch wrapped diagonally from his waist to his shoulder which contained a notebook and a couple pens. He was mostly a slate color with darker stripes on his forehead, back, and legs. The end of his thick tail was dark, as if it had been dipped in ink. The glasses, along with his stiff demeanor, gave the distinct impression of a snooty librarian. “Hello everyone. My name is-” 
“THAT'S IT!!!” a voice shouted. 
Everyone looked over at Dwayne. 
“I remembered it! I'm here as the Ultimate Scapegoat!” 
Dwayne seemed extremely pleased by this fact. The grey cat seemed extremely offended that - somehow - he had reminded Dwayne about an ability like that. 
“Why on earth would they accept you with a talent like that?” the ginger tabby to Grace's left scoffed. 
“I dunno. Ask him,” the Great Dane shrugged, gesturing to Axel, who merely smiled. It was decidedly an odd talent, (if it could even be called that), but somehow Grace could tell he was - at least to some extent - telling the truth. But he was still clearly hiding something. 
“Well… whatever,” the blue mackerel scoffed. Then he continued with his introduction. “My name is Thymes. I'm from the state of New York, and I'm the Ultimate Journalist.” Grace smiled inwardly. That explained a lot. “Back home I would always get the best stories. My column alone made my small town paper the best in the county. And all of it was true-to-goodness real news. You won't find these claws digging for dirt.” Thymes smirked, holding up a paw. 
Next was the Bernese mountain dog with the wheelchair harness. His fur was a complicated pattern of black, dark brown, and white. The harness wrapped around his waist was made from a dark, rugged leather. It connected to a complex metal frame which was coated in a shining black paint. On the left and right of the frame hung two pouches constructed from the same leather as his harness. There was a platform for his hind legs to rest on, and a suspension system connected to a large pair of wheels so he could maneuver freely. The canine seemed somewhat shy, but eager and excited to be there. “Hi everyone. I'm Rex and I’m the Ultimate Geologist. I’m from South Dakota. I got invited here for creating a model that explained a lot of the geological and tectonic activity back home. It makes for a good major, but what I really love doing is paleontology! It's the reason I got into geology in the first place!” He truly lit up as he mentioned palientology. Grace made a mental note to ask him about it later. 
Then it was the ginger tabby cat’s turn. He was a beautiful tanish-orange with patches of white on his nose, his chest, and his paws. Stripes of burnt sienna crossed his back and tail. When he spoke, his haughty tone and perpetual smirk gave the distinct impression that he thought he was better than everyone else. “Hey, everyone! I'm Wild and I'm the Ultimate Daredevil. Came over here from Nevada. I like doing all kinds of stunts, but paws down my absolute favorite are cycling stunts. Ran my own show and everything. That's what got the board paying attention to me.” 
As Wild started enumerating the complicated tricks that had caught the board’s attention, the black Labrador who had been dragged into the circle against his will leaned over to Grace. However, now that she had a closer look, she wasn't sure if he was a Lab, since he had pointed ears instead of the typical floppy ears that the breed normally had. In fact, now that he wasn’t caught by surprise, he seemed to be an entirely different dog. He came off as reserved and cautious, as though he felt like he was in a trap, and any false step could spring it. “What do I say? I don't think I'm supposed to be here,” he whispered hesitantly. 
“Join the club,” Grace sighed, letting a smile cross her face. 
“No, I mean actually. I can't remember having an ultimate talent or anything.” 
Grace’s eyes widened. This was beyond odd. How could not one, but two of the new students not know what they were even there for? And yet… Chase seemed to be expecting someone else. If this dog didn't know why he was here, and if he had the same experience that she had upon waking up, then maybe he really wasn't one of the new students. Maybe he had been ‘kidnapped’ by accident. She thought through the next course of action before responding. “With all the confusion so far, just make up something for now. We can figure out how to proceed later.” 
The black dog nodded in appreciation. Thankfully, he had a couple seconds to think before Wild finished talking. Then it was his turn. 
“Hi, everyone. My name is Gizmo,” he began. His wary hesitancy was gone, replaced by a mask of confidence. “I'm from Maine, and I'm the Ultimate Inventor. I got the title for inventing teleportation.” Grace raised her eyes. Of all the things she was expecting to hear, it wasn't something as outlandish as that. And yet… she felt like he was telling the truth. Either he believed he was telling the truth, or he was so good at bluffing that Grace couldn't tell if he was lying or not. 
“Teleportation?” Shade asked incredulously. “You invented teleportation?” 
Gizmo nodded. “I discovered how to manipulate spacetime using exotic matter to create stable Einstein-Rosen Bridges.” 
“There's no such thing as exotic matter,” the tuxedo cat objected. 
“Just because you've never seen it doesn't mean it doesn't exist,” the black Lab responded cooly.
